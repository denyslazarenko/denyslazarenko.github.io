        <li><b>Guide the opening</b> — a short audio prompt hands you a natural opener so you stay present while the research happens in the background.</li>
        <li><b>Capture the conversation</b> — speech-to-text transcribes and summarises each interaction into a searchable, follow-up-ready record.</li>

    <h3>Leleka Lens — human data for robot learning</h3>
    <p class="italic">January 2026</p>
    <p>A "Coursera for Robots": a global platform for collecting first-person human
    data for robot learning. People record everyday tasks — cooking, repairing,
    assembling, sorting — using smart glasses or a head-mounted phone, anonymise
    the footage on-device, and turn it into structured, privacy-preserving datasets
    that can train the next generation of robots, world models, and embodied AI.</p>
    <p>Real-world robot data is scarce, narrow, and expensive to collect, so robots
    struggle to generalise across homes, objects, and environments. Leleka Lens turns
    everyday human experience into a living dataset for robotics, and gives contributors
    a creator economy — they build thematic channels of hands-on footage and earn when
    their data is licensed or used in research.</p>

    <h3>Human pulse measurement with web camera</h3>
    <p class="italic">April 2015</p>
    <p class="note">
        <i class="fa fa-star"></i>
        University project
    </p>
    <p>Our pulse is the rate at which heart beats. As our heart pumps blood through the body, we can feel pulsing in some of the blood vessels close to the skin`s surface, such as in our head, neck or upper arm. To the human eye, no matter how long and hard you stare at your wrist or someone else’s face, you would struggle to detect a change in color. For a computer, however, the tiniest per-pixel fluctuations are easy to detect. As our heart pumps blood around body arteries swell with bright red blood, which changes the color of skin slightly. Thus we can measure accurately our heart rate. This project works by combining several techniques common in the field of computer vision.</p>
